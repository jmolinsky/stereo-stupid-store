Masquerade

This picture came from a Halloween party my wife (then girlfriend) and I attended at a friend's house way back in 1983. What made this evening so memorable, was that we kept our identities hidden from our friends for the better part of the evening. We still socialized with other guest, but we talked in coarse, old people voices. We were really surprised when others couldn't figure out who we were. We finally had to give ourselves up when friends started to become alarmed at our absence from the party:

"Where are they?" Asked Frankenstein.

"They definitely said they were coming," said Frankenstein's bride.

"Too bad, I wanted to suck their blood," said Dracula.

It was kind of fun being virtually invisible for a while. We got a rare opportunity to see how our friends acted when they didn't think we were around. Of course this was a dangerous game we were playing, and it could have backfired on us. Our friends might have said:

"Who cares where they are?"

"I never liked them anyway."

"No big loss...they aren't my blood type."

As it turned out, we weren't the only ones playing this game that night. Alone, in the corner stood a red devil pounding down drink after drink. It was assumed that like us, he too would eventually divulge his true identity...but he didn't. At the end of the evening, the party hostess finally asked him who he was.

"Oh, you don't know me," he said. "I was coming from a boring party down the road and saw your lights on. It looked like you guys were having a lot of fun, so I thought I'd drop in. Thanks for a great party."

He then dissappeared as mysteriously as he appeared.

HAPPY HALLOWEEN!

I recognize this song. It fills the lobby and I feel my heart settle. Maybe they’re warming up. They’re probably just checking the levels or whatever. God it sounds good on these speakers though. A little loud maybe, but this is us. This is that tune we’ve been playing for months. It just came to us note by note. We built it one piece at a time until there was nothing more to add.

Just then one of the Toms opens the door to the main hall. He’s coming towards me, he can tell me where to go! I open my mouth to ask, but he blows right by and heads for the concession stand. I push through the door and the music hits me like lead. It’s way louder in here. I can feel my ribs rattle. I stand there at the back of the room, getting my bearings. The stage is brimming with Toms. It can barely hold them all. They’re not rehearsing or warming up or soundchecking or whatever you wanna call it. They’re playing. And they sound like a real band. I feel my grip tighten and loosen on the handle of the guitar case. I look for some way around to the side of the stage or steps up to it. I just can’t see anything from this far away and I start weaving through the folks standing in front of me. I gently touch somebody on the shoulder to ask them if I can scoot by and they turn to face me, excitedly.

“Is this amazing or what?!”

I think that’s what he shouts. I’m a little confused that he’s also wearing the mask. And that’s when I scan the crowd and realize that nearly everybody has one. Someone must’ve brought the box from the office and handed them out. I can’t move. I’m stuck like that for who knows how long. The music finally brings me back. The song winds down and I see Bass Tom look up from his instrument. I hold up my guitar case and point at it. Did he just cock his head? Bass Tom, it’s me. Dammit.
