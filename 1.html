I don't know their names, their real names anyway, but we meet every Tuesday night. We're all Tom Peterson. Yes, that Tom Peterson. That poor guy who lost his appliance empire a few years back. My name is... no, that's not true anymore. I play guitar, poorly, and a little tambourine. I can barely see my fingers to make sure the chord shapes are right, but I'm starting to hear it in my head. We have to keep the lights low so nobody knows we're in here. This store's been closed since 2000 something. You can feel his spirit everywhere.

I catch the drummer's eyes through his mask and I try to lock in with his rhythm. Everything is muted because, again, we're technically trespassing. But even at this hushed volume, it's starting to make sense.

I hear the gradual build towards a chord change or a chorus... a dark rumble sweeping towards me. And then. The Tom on bass starts singing. Just like that. We've never had vocals before and it tears something open.

"Church camp love, be my bride, I listen for you, far and wide"

There's a hitch in the sound. Everyone seems momentarily off kilter, a half step slow, but thank god, we recover and keep going. Bass Tom is somewhere beyond and my eyes can't quite reach him no matter how I try. The rest of us just have to join him there. A wet cough cuts through, I hear it between the notes, but can’t find the source. It might as well be me or anyone else. We know what Gloria meant, how much Tom trusted her, adored her, and we all have our own version of that I’m sure. You just have to be a person in the world for any length of time.​
